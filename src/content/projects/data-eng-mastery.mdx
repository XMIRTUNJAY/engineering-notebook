## What it feels like to use

You sign up and answer a short onboarding quiz (your role, experience, exam date, daily minutes). The app builds a study plan across the weeks to your exam. Each day you open the dashboard: concepts to learn, flashcards due for review, your weakest topics ranked, a study streak. You read a concept — five explanation levels, examples, traps to avoid — take practice questions, and watch mastery scores climb. When ready, you generate a mock exam: fairly sampled across sections and difficulties, graded at 70%, XP and badges on pass. Behind it all, a content pipeline turns raw syllabi into this curriculum, and a knowledge graph answers "what should I learn first?"

## Why this shape

Three everyday pains drove the design: learners can't tell what to study next (so: recommendations engine), forget on a schedule nobody tracks (so: SM-2 reviews), and practice on unfair question sets (so: stratified exam assembly). A fourth pain belongs to the builder: content rots, so a gated pipeline with validators stands between raw material and learners.

<Callout type="decision" title="Why no AI in the learning loop">
  Exam answers must be reproducible and explainable. Every recommendation is arithmetic you can audit — same inputs, same outputs, free forever. Language models help only offline, drafting content behind seven validators.
</Callout>

## Constraints

- Must work as a complete study loop with no model calls at runtime.
- Reviews must follow memory science, not vibes (SM-2 with measured parameters).
- Exams must be fair: mixed difficulties, spread sections, seeded shuffles.
- Content must pass gates before learners see it (schema → code checks).
- One person must be able to operate it (single database, Compose locally).

## Engineering decisions

<Decision
  title="Arithmetic recommendations instead of an LLM"
  problem="Learners need next-step guidance that is consistent, explainable, and free."
  options={["LLM-driven suggestions", "Weighted deterministic algorithms", "Fixed curriculum order"]}
  chosen="Six pure-Python modules: mastery, SM-2, weakness, recommendations, paths, exam assembly."
  why="Same inputs give same outputs; every formula has unit tests; a recommendation can be explained coefficient by coefficient."
  tradeoffs="No understanding of free-text answers; weights are judgment frozen in code, never A/B tested. I would choose learned ranking only with logged outcomes to train on."
  result="86 unit tests in ~4 seconds; recommendations debuggable line by line."
/>

<Decision
  title="One Postgres for rows, vectors, and text"
  problem="Content needs relational queries, similarity search, and keyword search without operating three systems."
  options={["Postgres + pgvector + FTS", "Postgres + external vector DB", "Separate search service"]}
  chosen="Postgres 16 with pgvector HNSW, tsvector GIN, JSONB content columns."
  why="One backup, one migration chain, one pool — and cross-workload joins stay free."
  tradeoffs="The 1024-dimension contract: changing embedding models migrates data. I would split past single-node vector scale or with an independent search team."
  result="Semantic search is one indexed SQL query beside everything else."
/>

## What didn't work

Resolved, evidenced items only:

**Client-awarded XP.** The app once trusted the browser to grant rewards; browsers can lie. Evidence: the BUG-017 comment in `exams.py`, now awarding `50 + round(score * 50)` server-side on pass. Lesson: name the source of truth per concern.

**Untested math.** The 2025-09-01 audit found no test framework. The engine split plus 9 test files followed — 86 passing, engine modules at 93–100% each. Lesson: untested code isn't just risky, it's unteachable.

**Polluted demo data.** E2E fixture rows were found sitting in production-shape data. Lesson: seed and teardown are part of the test, not decoration.
